# Rice Cultivation Cost and Profit per Bigha in Bangladesh: Boro and Aman Budget Examples

**Quick answer:** There is no single reliable cost or profit figure for growing rice on one bigha of land. The result depends on the season (Boro or Aman), variety, irrigation, wages, land costs, saleable yield and actual farmgate price. This worked example assumes **33 decimals in one bigha**: cash expenses of **BDT 19,500**, economic costs of **BDT 24,000**, saleable paddy of **650 kg**, paddy price of **BDT 32/kg**, and **BDT 2,000** in straw revenue. It produces **BDT 3,300 in cash surplus**, but an **economic loss of BDT 1,200** after the value of family labour and owned land is included.

**All example prices, costs and yields are hypothetical educational inputs**, not verified average Bangladesh prices or a forecast for the 2026 growing season.

## What does one bigha mean?

This article uses **1 bigha = 33 decimals** as a common Bangladesh planning convention. A decimal (shotok) is 1/100 acre or 435.6 square feet. Definitions of bigha can differ by locality: calculate with your own measured land area before comparing costs and production.

**Cash surplus is not the same as full economic profit.** Owned land and family labour may have economic value even when no cash leaves the farm.

## Boro versus Aman: why costs differ

Boro cultivation commonly takes place in the dry season, when irrigation can be a major cost. Aman cultivation may use more seasonal rainfall, but drought spells, heavy rain, flooding, soil conditions and labour costs still matter. Neither season is always cheaper or more profitable on every field.

Before choosing a season or variety, review the [Bangladesh Agrometeorological Information Service (BAMIS)](https://www.bamis.gov.bd/croppnp/1/all/75) for relevant advisories and consult the local extension office.

## Four calculations every farmer should know

### 1. Paddy sale revenue

**Revenue from paddy = saleable paddy (kg) × actual selling price (BDT/kg)**.

In the example: 650 × 32 = **BDT 20,800**.

### 2. Total revenue, including by-products

**Total revenue = paddy sale revenue + actual by-product revenue**.

In the example: 20,800 + 2,000 = **BDT 22,800**. If straw is not sold, do not automatically invent a sale. If it is consumed on the farm, account for its economic value consistently and never double-count it.

### 3. Cash surplus versus economic return

**Cash surplus = total revenue − cash expenses = BDT 3,300**.

**Economic return = total revenue − (cash expenses + imputed family labour + land opportunity cost) = −BDT 1,200**.

### 4. Cost per kilogram of saleable paddy

**Cash production cost per kg = cash expenses ÷ saleable paddy (kg)**.

In the example: 19,500 ÷ 650 = **BDT 30/kg**. Economic cost per kg: 24,000 ÷ 650 ≈ **BDT 36.92/kg**. These gross unit-cost measures do not yet deduct by-product revenue.

## What happens if yield or price changes?

Here all scenarios assume the same BDT 19,500 cash expenses, BDT 24,000 economic expenses, and BDT 2,000 straw income for illustration.

| Scenario | Saleable paddy | Price/kg | Total revenue | Cash surplus | Economic return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unfavourable | 550 kg | BDT 30 | BDT 18,500 | −BDT 1,000 | −BDT 5,500 |
| Base case | 650 kg | BDT 32 | BDT 22,800 | BDT 3,300 | −BDT 1,200 |
| Favourable | 750 kg | BDT 35 | BDT 28,250 | BDT 8,750 | BDT 4,250 |

These scenarios are **not actual local yield estimates or market quotations**. Some costs can vary with actual output; revise them in your own forecast.

## Break-even price: how much must paddy sell for?

If straw revenue is included, an illustrative break-even paddy price is:

**(Total relevant costs − by-product income) ÷ saleable paddy (kg)**.

For 650 kg and BDT 2,000 straw income:

- Cash-cost break-even: (19,500 − 2,000) ÷ 650 ≈ **BDT 26.92/kg**.
- Full economic break-even: (24,000 − 2,000) ÷ 650 ≈ **BDT 33.85/kg**.
- If there is no straw income, cash break-even becomes **BDT 30/kg**.

Use final **saleable weight**, not total harvest weight before drying and grading.

## Is the government's Aman procurement price the same as the market price?

**No.** The Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha reported on **November 10, 2026** that the government's 2026–27 Aman procurement price was set at **BDT 35/kg for paddy**. This is a price for a particular public procurement programme—not a guarantee that every grower can sell any paddy at that price. Eligibility, timing, grade and procurement arrangements matter. See [BSS's original report](https://www.bssnews.net/news/444159) and compare local market information via the [Department of Agricultural Marketing](https://market.dam.gov.bd/?L=E).

Do not substitute retail milled-rice prices for farmgate paddy prices.

## Steps to improve the quality of your own budget

1. Confirm suitable rice variety, season and field conditions with local agricultural guidance.
2. Record irrigation frequency, fuel, electricity and water charges.
3. Calculate fertilizer requirements using soil and crop recommendations rather than arbitrary rates; see [IRRI Bangladesh Nutrient Manager](https://webapps.irri.org/bd/nmr/index.html).
4. Compare labour and equipment costs, including harvesting and threshing.
5. Record storage and drying losses and the quantity actually sold.
6. Separate government procurement prices from local farmgate offers.
7. Value unpaid family labour and owned land when calculating economic returns.

Try AGRIVA's [agriculture calculators](https://agriva.com.bd/en/agriculture-calculators) for planning; confirm your inputs against field records.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does one bigha of rice cultivation cost?

There is no reliable fixed amount. This article's BDT 19,500 is a hypothetical cash-cost example, not a district or national average.

### How much profit can I make per bigha?

Estimate actual revenue, by-product income, cash expenses and opportunity costs. The example illustrates why a positive cash surplus can coexist with an economic loss.

### Is Boro always more expensive than Aman?

No. Boro may be more irrigation-dependent, but local labour, weather, variety and fuel prices can alter the comparison.

### Do I need to count family labour and owned land?

For a full economic comparison, yes; for cashflow planning, show actual cash outlays separately.

### What is a useful break-even calculation?

Subtract genuine by-product income from relevant costs and divide by saleable paddy weight. Document every assumption.
