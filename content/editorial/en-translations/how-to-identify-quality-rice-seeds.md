# How to Identify Quality Rice Seed in Bangladesh: 9 Checks and a Germination Test

**Quick answer:** Good-quality rice seed should be of the right variety for the intended season and field, come from a traceable source, have suitable germination, physical purity, seed health and storage conditions, and carry accurate label and seed-lot information. A seed that looks plump or clean is not necessarily viable or genetically pure.

This is an English editorial adaptation of AGRIVA's [published Bengali rice-seed quality guide](https://agriva.com.bd/blog/how-to-identify-quality-rice-seeds). Where official seed-class standards apply, follow those standards rather than treating an informal home test as certification.

## At a glance: reassuring and warning signs

| What to inspect | Reassuring evidence | Reason to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variety and season | Traceable named variety suitable for the season | Ambiguous claims such as "high-yield rice" without a variety |
| Physical cleanliness | Little visible foreign material or mixed seeds | Straw, weeds, dirt or obvious mixed grain |
| Seed fill | Healthy-looking, normally filled grain | Many broken, empty or badly damaged grains |
| Germination | Appropriate lot-specific test information | Low or undocumented normal seedling emergence |
| Seed health | No obvious mould, insect damage or unusual odour | Visible mould, live insects, clumping or rotting smell |
| Storage | Suitable drying and storage history | Wet packaging, damage or unknown storage |
| Traceability | Producer, lot number and appropriate label details | Incomplete label or claims that cannot be checked |

**Important:** Appearance cannot prove germination percentage, freedom from invisible pathogens or genetic purity.

## 1. Select the correct variety and season

The first quality check is variety identity. Even viable seed can perform poorly when planted in unsuitable local conditions or a different production season. Bangladesh has Boro, Aman and Aus rice-growing seasons, and variety recommendations can differ by location, soil, water conditions and weather risk.

Consult the [Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI)](https://brri.gov.bd/) and local agricultural extension service for variety and season suitability.

## 2. Check the source and relevant certification

Buy from a traceable producer, authorised dealer or credible seed supplier. When a seed category uses a certification tag, check whether the tag is genuine and matches the particular seed lot. Do not assume the same certification requirements apply to all seed categories.

Bangladesh's [Seed Certification Agency](https://sca.gov.bd/) operates seed-testing services. A seller's claim or online product listing alone is not proof of lot-specific quality.

## 3. Read the packaging and lot information

Review the crop and variety name, lot number, seed quantity, producer, test or packing date, and any relevant purity and germination information. A damaged, relabelled or old package deserves extra scrutiny. Ask to see the actual rear label where important information may appear.

Keep the invoice, lot reference and seller details in case questions arise after purchase.

## 4. Look for physical impurities and mixtures

Place a representative sample on a clean, light-coloured surface. Note visible foreign material, weed seed and obviously different grains. **Physical purity** and **varietal/genetic purity** are separate concepts; visual inspection alone cannot confirm either to laboratory standards.

## 5. Examine grain fill and visible damage

Look for a high proportion of normally developed, filled grains and check for visibly broken, hollow, damaged or discoloured material. Natural appearance varies by rice variety—including pigmented rice. Colour alone does not determine whether a seed is good or bad.

Even well-filled grain may fail a germination test.

## 6. Check germination, not just whether the seed cracks

An appropriate seed germination test considers how many seeds produce **normal seedlings**, not simply how many show a small root tip. BRRI's [rice production training materials](https://www.knowledgebank-brri.org/Rice_Production_Training_Manual/Day_1/Module_3/Factsheet%204.pdf) explain simple seed-testing approaches.

Germination requirements depend on the seed type and applicable standards. The commonly cited **80% or greater** germination figure in BRRI farmer guidance should not automatically be presented as a universal legal threshold for every seed category.

## 7. Watch for mould, insects and abnormal odours

Visible fungal growth, insects, insect holes, wet clumps or rotting odours are warnings. However, seed that looks clean can still carry hidden pathogens. Seek qualified testing or agricultural advice if a lot is doubtful.

**Safety:** Follow the handling instructions for chemically treated seed; avoid touching or soaking treated seed contrary to label directions.

## 8. Confirm drying and storage conditions

Seed viability can decline under poor moisture and temperature conditions. Ask about drying, packaging and storage. A Bangladesh agricultural information guide discusses reducing moisture before storage, but **moisture percentage cannot be accurately judged by touch**—a suitable meter or laboratory measurement is required.

Do not promise that all stored seed remains usable for a fixed number of months; check seed age, conditions and germination.

## 9. Keep proof of purchase and check seller support

Record the producer, seller, variety, lot, quantity, price, purchase date and contact details. Ask about return or complaint procedures before buying online. A listing on [AGRIVA Marketplace](https://agriva.com.bd/marketplace) does not itself certify seed quality or guarantee yield.

## A simple 100-seed home germination check

A household test can provide a preliminary indication before sowing. It is **not** an official certified seed-laboratory result.

1. Draw **100 seeds** randomly from different portions of the **same lot** rather than selecting only attractive grains.
2. Place them separately on clean moist paper or cloth in suitable conditions, following crop-specific guidance where available.
3. Keep the medium moist but do not leave seeds submerged without oxygen.
4. Protect the sample from contamination, harsh sun and unsuitable temperatures.
5. Count seedlings that meet the relevant **normal seedling** criteria at the recommended observation time.
6. Record the sample size, normal seedling count, date and conditions. Re-test or seek an official service if the outcome is doubtful.

**Germination percentage = (normal seedlings ÷ seeds tested) × 100.**

| Seeds tested | Normal seedlings | Calculation | Preliminary result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 92 | 92 ÷ 100 × 100 | 92% |
| 100 | 86 | 86 ÷ 100 × 100 | 86% |
| 100 | 72 | 72 ÷ 100 × 100 | 72%; investigate further |

For example, **86 normal seedlings from 100 tested seeds = 86%** in this sample. This does not prove the same percentage of established plants in the field.

### Home test versus laboratory certification

| Feature | Home screening | Official laboratory testing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Preliminary personal indication | Standardised testing for the relevant purpose |
| Sample | For example, one 100-seed batch | Prescribed sampling and replication |
| Germination | Simplified observation | Normal seedlings under standard conditions |
| Purity | Visible foreign material | Tested using defined laboratory methods |
| Record | Farmer's own notes | Formal test report where applicable |

Bangladesh's Seed Certification Agency describes laboratory germination, purity and other seed tests. **Do not market a single informal home test as an official certificate.**

## Does sinking or floating in water identify good seed?

**Not reliably.** Floating grains may sometimes indicate poor filling, but sinking alone does not prove viability, freedom from disease or genetic purity. Use approved seed-selection methods appropriate to the variety and do not soak chemically treated seed without authorisation.

## Can farmers save seed from their own rice harvest?

Seed saving may be possible with suitable varieties, but maintain varietal identity, avoid mixtures, harvest from healthy fields, dry and store correctly, and test germination before planting. The next generation of hybrid rice may not maintain the same uniformity or performance as the original hybrid crop.

## Buying checklist

- Correct **variety, season and regional suitability**
- Traceable producer, seed **lot number** and appropriate packaging
- Documented **germination information**, where available
- Physical purity and no obvious pests, mould or damage
- Suitable **storage and handling** history
- Clear instructions if seed is chemically treated
- Seller contact, invoice and complaint arrangements
- Seed quantity calculated from the chosen crop and planting method

AGRIVA's [agriculture calculators](https://agriva.com.bd/en/agriculture-calculators) can help with planning, but confirm appropriate seed rates with local experts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a clean-looking grain necessarily good rice seed?

No. Lot identity, germination, purity, seed health and suitable storage also matter.

### How is a 100-seed germination percentage calculated?

Count normal seedlings, divide by the total tested and multiply by 100. If 85 normal seedlings emerge from 100 tested seeds, the sample's result is **85%**.

### Is the same germination percentage legally required for all seed?

No. Check the standards applicable to the specific seed category and official testing procedure.

### Does sinking in water prove good germination?

No; floating or sinking offers at most a limited indication.

### Is a certification tag sufficient for a damaged package?

Confirm the tag, seed lot and storage history; damaged packaging or doubtful storage may still warrant testing.

### Will saved hybrid rice seed give the same result the next season?

Not necessarily. Hybrid progeny can segregate and yield or traits may be less uniform.
